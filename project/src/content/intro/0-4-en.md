## Introduction

After the earlier pieces, you may still ask: **what is this subscription actually doing?** Not to master a method overnight, but to see what kind of content you're following.

## At a Glance

- **What the subscription does**: put **questions of the moment** together with **classics and serious books you've read**, and publish **articles you can look up and discuss**.
- **We call it**: **ideas in collision**—question meets material, judgment emerges; not a pile of excerpts.
- **AI's role**: help us **write clearly** what collision produced; **not** choose the question or make the judgment.
- **In one line**: you subscribe to **views after collision**, not **unchecked summaries**.

## Ideas in Collision: What This Subscription Practices

**Ideas in collision** is our name for this way of writing.

Each piece starts with an **observation point**—one question worth clarifying now. From what we've read we take **factual basis**—passages you can cite and retell. The two **collide**: if they connect, we write a **view**; if not, we'd rather admit we haven't worked it out than paste pretty words.

So this subscription is **not** a roundup of book reviews, **not** hot-take punditry, **not** AI-generated long copy handed to you as-is.  
**It is**: a study that keeps doing "**question × material → judgment**."

If you've read the first three intro articles, you know how we read and draft behind the scenes; **this piece only states what we deliver outward: articles after collision.**

## An Example: What Collision Looks Like

We often cite **Ralph Waldo Emerson** (American essayist and thinker, 1803–1882). In "The American Scholar" he says books record past minds; their value is to **inspire** thought today—"in comes fact, out comes thought," not training people only to paraphrase.

**This subscription is a contemporary version of that**: take **factual basis** from one or several books, collide it with **one observation point today**, and publish a piece **we'll sign and you can trace to sources**. Emerson states the ideal; we **practice** it in weekly articles.

## What AI Does in This Subscription

AI does **not** run "collision"—which observation point, whether material connects, whether the view holds, is for people.

AI **drafts**: paragraphs, transitions, wording—turning what is **already collided** into prose you can read.  
Skip collision and leave only AI layout, however smooth, and it's not what this subscription delivers.

## Conclusion

**Ideas in collision** = the core of this subscription: **hit material with a question, write judgment open to correction.** You follow **articles that went through collision**; AI only makes them **easier to read**, not **more true**.
