## Introduction

The [previous article](/en/intro/0-1-en) explained that writing for an **observation point** reveals **factual basis** you thought you understood but hadn't connected. Many people stall on the next step—not **no ideas**, but **fear of writing**: bad prose, messy structure, a third draft that still doesn't look right.

My split is simple: **I read and I think; AI polishes language and organizes what I've already thought.** That's not laziness; it's spending effort only where you must.

## At a Glance

- **Read**: input and check (which factual basis fits the observation point).
- **Think**: observation point, trade-offs, judgment—**not outsourceable**.
- **Draft** (formatting): paragraphs, transitions, wording—**can go to AI**.
- **Bottom line**: AI arranges what you've already thought; empty head in, empty talk out.

## "Drafting" and "Thinking" Were Never the Same Job

I separate **composing** (problem sense, insight that rewires your mind) from **drafting** (rhetoric, structure, compliant phrasing): **drafting can go to AI; composing stays with you.** Using composing bandwidth on punctuation is third-draft hell.

**Arthur Schopenhauer** (German philosopher, 1788–1860), in "On Authorship," sorts authors: some **think while they write**, some **think first, then write**—the latter are rare, but like a hunt: game is circled first, then released one by one to the page, "hardly escaping." What you hand to AI is that **release and clarify** step; you set the bounds of the hunt.

## Writing Will Not Answer for You

**Plato** (ancient Greek philosopher, c. 427–347 BCE), in the *Phaedrus*, has Socrates say: if writing is questioned, **it does not answer**; once fixed, it passes among those who understand and those who don't. Another kind of discourse is **written in the learner's soul**, knowing when to speak and when to stop. AI paragraphs are more like the former—fluent, complete, yet they won't carry your judgment.

So I never ask AI to "write me a piece about some observation point." I decide first: what is the observation point, which factual bases to connect, what I reject—then ask AI to **organize the thought and polish the words**.

## Read a Lot—Still Light Your Own Fire

**Schopenhauer**, in "On Thinking for Oneself," says reading presses **foreign thoughts** onto the mind like a seal on wax; **thinking for yourself** ties to temperament and circumstance now. Anyone can read; **thinking must be lit**—usually by an observation point you actually care about. That's why the [previous article](/en/intro/0-1-en) said writing for an observation point exposes disconnected factual basis: without an observation point, more reading is only someone else's wax seal.

My habit now: after reading, a short list only (observation point, counterexamples, factual bases to connect), **dictate or bullet for AI**, ask it to shape paragraphs, cut filler, add transitions—I read once and change only **where the judgment is wrong**, not spar over style.

## You Still Own the Content

**Bertrand Russell** (British philosopher, 1872–1970), in *Philosophy*, says everyday "knowledge" is often overconfident and vague; we need **judgment open to correction**. AI can make sentences easier to read, but the **publisher** is still whoever must answer if observation point and factual basis are wired right or wrong—that's why I don't outsource thinking.

## Conclusion

Don't fear writing; fear **handing in before you've thought it through**. Reading and thinking are your job; polish and layout for AI is reasonable. The next piece covers **evidence → digest → view**—after you have an observation point and factual basis, how the middle layer of **composing** runs.
